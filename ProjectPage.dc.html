Project 03 Spring 2025

Blaze (V3)

A VEX High Stakes competition robot for Team 1138B

VEX Robotics Ranked top 5 in Southern California
VEX High Stakes Pneumatics CNC polycarbonate

Overview

Blaze is the third iteration of our competitive robot for VEX High Stakes. It was built around four goals: accelerate fast enough to win the neutral goal rush, keep that speed while carrying a mobile goal, pick up rings by driving over them, and score on mobile goals and wall stakes consistently.

[Add one sentence on your role.]

Team1138B
GameVEX High Stakes
ResultTournament finalist

Design goals

Drivetrain

Fast acceleration, and no noticeable loss in speed while holding a mobile goal.

Intake

Touch-it-own-it, with a large pickup area that needs very little alignment, able to score on mobile goals and feed the wall stake system.

Wall stake

Score quickly and efficiently.

Mobile goal manipulator

Pick up goals from any angle and hold them in one consistent position.

Specs

Drive6 motors, 600 rpm blue cartridges, 450 rpm output, 2.75 in wheels (omni, traction, omni)
Intake1 motor, 600 rpm output, 2 in flex wheels, hook stage on 12 to 18 tooth sprockets
Mobile goal clamp2 pistons, 25 mm stroke, no lever, single-way actuation with a band that pulls it back to release
Wall stake arm1 motor, 200 rpm green cartridge, 40 rpm output, friction-fit claw
Extras75 mm piston doinker, CNC-cut polycarbonate funnel

Design decisions

01
Gears cut to fit inside the wheels

Using 2.75 in wheels meant cutting our gears to fit inside the omni wheel. That gave a drive 5 holes wide with the same spacing as V1.

02
Low, centered intake

The intake motor sits low and near the center of the robot to keep the center of mass down. The tread with mounting holes has less friction and slop than other chain types, so we used it, and we built the hook stage off the robot first so the friction could be tuned without working around everything else.

03
A simpler clamp

Two standoff sets do the work: the first keeps the mobile goal in, the second tilts it into a consistent scoring position. A band pulls the clamp back to release, so the pistons only push one way.

04
Wall stake arm

Two arms sit on 60 tooth cut gears on a high-strength shaft and are braced to each other. The claw is polycarbonate with zip-tie mesh and a rubber band for grip.

05
Light, strong structure

The frame is built from angle, with the drive cross brace doubling as the intake mount. Anything that takes hits from other robots is boxed.

Testing

Drive

Good acceleration and top speed, a good turning feel from the center traction wheels, and much better performance with a full mobile goal than V1.

Intake

Easy to drive through a field of rings, consistent and fast on back-to-back scoring. The hook stage reached level 1 overheating after nearly ten minutes of constant use, and rings sometimes hit the hooks at the wrong time and delay the transition slightly.

Clamp

Once a goal is clamped it is nearly impossible to pull out, and it aligns at every angle, a big improvement on V1.

Project 04 Spring 2024

Breakthrough 2.0 (V6)

A VEX Over Under competition robot for Team 1138B

VEX Robotics Ranked top 5 in Southern California
VEX Over Under Pneumatics Custom plastics

Overview

V6 was built to fix what went wrong at our last tournament: slow scoring, a bent drivetrain, an unreliable hang, and a heavy robot. It is lighter, faster, and built around a ram-in scoring strategy.

I co-designed the base, intake, kicker, and hang, built the robot, and wrote the build documentation.

Team1138B
GameVEX Over Under
Result5th at SoCal regionals

Specs

Drive6 motors, 600 rpm cartridges, 450 rpm output (36:48), 6 omni and 2 custom traction wheels (2.75 in)
IntakeOver-the-top, 1 motor (600 rpm), 2 in flex wheels, chain and sprockets
Kicker1 motor (100 rpm cartridge), 20 rpm output, triple slip gear
HangVertical, 4 x 75 mm pistons, C-tier
Wings2 x 50 mm pistons, 28 in wingspan

Design decisions

01
Weight

Nylon hardware wherever the load is light, and double-stacked custom plastic bracing. A second full-length crossbrace fixed the bending we saw on V5 (about 16 lb).

02
Ram-in intake

A ziptie platform sits 3 holes back from the front, and a ziptie hardstop holds the intake at the right height. The triball rides on top, so the goal strips it out and scores in one push. Shortened from 18 to 15 holes for a better hold.

03
Kicker

Back to a kicker from a flywheel, based on our V3 design. Form-fitting plastic holders self-align each triball. The prototype cleared the bar 12 of 12 shots.

04
Hang

75 mm pistons with revised tower geometry give a consistent C-tier hang.

05
Wings

Drop-down wings, each on its own 50 mm piston to save air. Edge spikes help unload the matchload zone for the autonomous win point.

Project 01

6 DOF Arm (Armie)

A quiet desk robot that tidies up and hands me things while I work.

In progress
15% complete
SolidWorks NEMA 14 stepper

Overview

Armie is a six-degree-of-freedom robotic arm designed to live on my desk. The idea is a desk buddy that helps me tidy up and hands me things while I'm working. I'm designing it in SolidWorks, and it's still early: I'm building it from the gripper outward.

The goal

Quiet is the main requirement. An arm sitting next to me all day can't be noisy, so that shapes the choice of actuators and mechanisms. The other goals are being useful: putting things away and handing objects to me.

Done so far

01

Designed the end effector: a spiral cam gripper with two jaws on a linear rail, driven by a NEMA 14 stepper motor.

02

Modeled it in SolidWorks and checked the open and closed positions.

In progress

01

Refining the gripper design.

Still to do

01

Design the remaining joints and links to reach six degrees of freedom.

02

Choose and design quiet actuators for each joint.

03

Design the base and mount for the desk.

04

Electronics and motor control.

05

Build, assemble, and test.

06

Program the tidy and handoff behaviors.

Project 02 Winter 2025 – Spring 2026

Mach 5

A VEX competition robot for Team 1138B, my senior-year build

VEX Robotics Ranked top 5 at a national level event with a couple of Chinese teams
VEX Odometry 12-piece plastic budget

Overview

Mach 5 was built during my senior year, in the middle of application season, so the CAD and documentation are lighter than my earlier robots. It performed well and consistently, but we didn't adapt to how the game's meta shifted, and we fell short of the winning streak we'd had the two years before.

[Add one sentence on your role.]

Team1138B
SeasonSenior year

Design constraint

We were limited to 12 pieces of plastic for the whole season, so every cut had to earn its place. Plastic went only where metal couldn't do the job.

Specs

Drive6 motors, 450 rpm, 3.65 in wheels
OdometryLeaf-spring-mounted tracking wheels
Plastic budget12 pieces total

Results

Competed on the 4th-seeded alliance at the Diamond in the Desert Signature Event, a national event with international teams. Ranked 29th in qualifications (6-4), won the round of 16, and lost in the quarterfinals.

What I learned

I learned that CAD is useful, but spending too much time in it holds you back. Building and testing in the real world matters more, because even with all my experience, there were things CAD never showed me until the robot was in front of me.
